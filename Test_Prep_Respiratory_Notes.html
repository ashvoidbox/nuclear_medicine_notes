Test Topics Overview

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16 📗 NMP Basics · Ch. 5
Key test areas for Respiratory System Procedures:
1. A&P of the Respiratory System  ·  2. Radiopharmaceuticals (¹³³Xe, ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA, ⁹⁹ᵐTc-MAA)  ·  3. Imaging Protocols (Ventilation & Perfusion)  ·  4. V/Q Scan Interpretation (PE vs COPD)  ·  5. Lung Quantification

This guide consolidates content from the Respiratory System Procedures SoftChalk notes, NM&MI Technology and Techniques (Gilmore & Waterstram-Rich, Ch. 16), and Nuclear Medicine Physics: The Basics (Chandra & Rahmim, Ch. 5). Use the quiz to test yourself across all five areas.

The Big Picture

  • Nuclear medicine lung imaging uses two complementary scans: Ventilation (V) and Perfusion (Q)
  • Together they are called a V/Q scan
  • The primary clinical question is: Does this patient have a Pulmonary Embolism (PE)?
  • The key interpretive principle: a mismatched defect (normal V, abnormal Q) = PE; a matched defect (both V and Q abnormal) = COPD or obstructive disease
Ventilation: follows airflow  |  Perfusion: follows blood flow. Compare the same view side-by-side to identify matches vs. mismatches.

A&P Essentials

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16

Respiratory System Structure

  • Upper: Nose & Pharynx
  • Lower: Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Lungs

Bronchial Tree

  • Trachea → Primary Bronchi (R & L) → Secondary Bronchi → Tertiary Bronchi → Terminal Bronchioles
  • Right primary bronchus: more vertical, shorter, wider — foreign objects lodge here more easily
  • Right lung: 3 lobes, 3 secondary bronchi
  • Left lung: 2 lobes, 2 secondary bronchi (cardiac notch on left)

The Lungs

  • Right lung: thicker, broader, shorter (liver elevates right diaphragm); ~55% of total function
  • Left lung: narrower (cardiac notch); ~45% of total function
  • Alveoli (250–300 million) are the site of gas exchange
  • Pleural membranes surround each lung; pleural fluid reduces friction

Important Landmarks

StructureFunction / Significance
EpiglottisTrap door; covers trachea during swallowing; routes food to esophagus
HilusWhere bronchi, blood vessels, lymphatics, and nerves enter/exit the lung
Cardiac notchLeft lung; shows as attenuated area on CXR and lung scan
AlveoliGas exchange by diffusion across alveolar cells and capillary walls
We breathe automatically to eliminate CO₂, not in response to low O₂. Before inspiration, lung pressure = atmospheric pressure (760 mmHg). Diaphragm contracts → pressure drops → air rushes in.

Lung Pathology

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16

Key Conditions

ConditionDefinition
PneumothoraxAir in the pleural cavity; can cause lung collapse (atelectasis)
HemothoraxBlood in the pleural cavity
PleurisyInflammation of pleural membrane; causes chest/back pain from friction
PneumoniaAcute infection/inflammation of alveoli; filled with fluid and dead WBCs
EmphysemaDestruction of alveolar walls; creates air spaces; reduces gas exchange
BronchitisInflammation of bronchi; excessive mucus; chronic cough
AsthmaInflammatory disorder; difficulty breathing; NOT caused by smoking

COPD

  • Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease — obstruction/narrowing of air passages
  • 80% caused by smoking; 20% air pollutants
  • Types: Asthma, Bronchitis, Emphysema
  • S&S: cough, breathlessness, wheezing, dyspnea

Pulmonary Embolism (PE)

  • Blood clot (or other foreign material) in a pulmonary vessel — obstructs circulation
  • Most originate from DVT (deep vein thrombosis), usually in the legs
  • Risk factors: surgery, cancer, pregnancy, smoking, birth control pills, prolonged immobility
  • S&S: sudden chest pain, dyspnea, cyanosis, tachycardia, shock

Three Types of PE

  • Distal PE: Small clot in lung periphery; pleuritic chest pain
  • Lobar PE: Clot in lobar branch; blocks flow to one lobe
  • Saddle PE: Massive clot straddling the bifurcation of the main pulmonary artery — life-threatening
Treatment of PE: (1) anticoagulants (Heparin) to prevent new clots, (2) thrombolytics (Streptokinase) to dissolve clots, (3) surgical clot removal, (4) IVC filter placement.

Radiopharmaceuticals

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16 📗 NMP Basics · Ch. 5

Two Categories

  • Ventilation agents — follow airflow
  • Perfusion agents — follow blood flow

Ventilation Agents

AgentTypeStatus
¹³³Xe (Xenon-133)Radioactive inert gasMost commonly used; byproduct of fission → cheap
⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA aerosolRadiolabeled aerosolCommon alternative; uses nebulizer
¹²⁷XeRadioactive inert gasExpensive; not readily available — historical
⁸¹ᵐKr (Krypton)Radioactive inert gasT½ = 13 sec; needs on-site generator — inconvenient

Perfusion Agents

AgentMechanismDose
⁹⁹ᵐTc-MAA (Macroaggregated Albumin)Capillary blockade — particles lodge in pulmonary capillaries on 1st pass1–5 mCi IV
⁹⁹ᵐTc-PulmoliteSimilar to MAA; interchangeable1–5 mCi IV

MAA Key Facts

  • Particle size: 5–100 µm (most: 10–30 µm)
  • Minimum particles: 100,000; typically inject 200,000–700,000
  • 95% localize in lungs on first pass
  • Blocks 1 in 1,000 normal capillaries — all others remain open for O₂
Inject ⁹⁹ᵐTc-MAA slowly over 30 seconds with patient supine. Do NOT flush the syringe with blood — both factors cause particle clumping.

¹³³Xe Imaging Protocol

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16

Setup

  • Load 10–20 mCi of ¹³³Xe into a gas generator
  • Patient positioned supine or upright, camera posterior
  • Patient connected to facemask or mouthpiece
  • Only the POST view is taken (least attenuation; best lung field view)

Three Phases

  1. Wash-in (Single Breath / Initial Breath)
    • Patient exhales maximally, then inhales ¹³³Xe and holds for 15 sec
    • Static image taken
    • Represents regional ventilation
    • Detects 66% of ventilation defects
  2. Equilibrium
    • Patient rebreathes expired ¹³³Xe with 2 L of O₂ for 2–5 minutes in a closed system
    • Static image taken
    • Least sensitive for defects; essential baseline for washout
    • Represents distribution of lung volume
  3. Washout Phase
    • ¹³³Xe stopped; patient breathes fresh air/O₂ for 2–3 minutes
    • 15-second serial images taken
    • ¹³³Xe should clear within 2–3 min
    • Retention = air trapping / obstructive disease
    • Detects 90% of obstructive airway diseases
Total ¹³³Xe study takes approximately 15 minutes. Patient can move directly to the perfusion scan — staying on the imaging table.

⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA Aerosol Protocol

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16

Procedure Steps

  1. Load 30 mCi of ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA into a nebulizer in 2–3 mL volume
  2. Connect oxygen to the nebulizer at 10 L/min — O₂ picks up DTPA to form a radiolabeled aerosol
  3. Administer aerosol via mouthpiece; patient supine for even distribution
  4. Patient breathes for 5–10 minutes
    • 0.5–1.0 mCi localizes in lungs
    • 150K cpm should localize
  5. Images taken with patient upright or supine
  6. Take 6 images for 200K–300K counts: ANT, POST, R & L LAT, POST obliques
Only 2–10% of the 30 mCi dose actually reaches the patient's lungs. This is expected and normal — not a concern.

Perfusion Imaging Protocol

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16 📗 NMP Basics · Ch. 5

Procedure Steps

  1. Inject 1–5 mCi of ⁹⁹ᵐTc-MAA IV with patient supine
    • Inject slowly over 30 seconds
    • Do NOT flush syringe with blood — causes particle clumping
  2. Take 6 images for 500K counts: ANT, POST, R & L LAT, POST obliques
    • POST is the most important view
    • For LAT views: have patient put arms above their head to limit attenuation

Interpretation Baseline

  • Ventilation scan is performed first in a V/Q study
  • Compare each perfusion view with the same view from ventilation
  • Look for defects that are present on perfusion but not ventilation
Remain consistent with your count goals across patients — 500K for perfusion, 200–300K for aerosol ventilation.

Interpreting V/Q Scans

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16

The Core Rule

Mismatched defect = Normal V + Abnormal Q → Pulmonary Embolism (PE) Matched defect = Abnormal V + Abnormal Q → COPD / Obstructive Disease

Probability Classifications

FindingPE Probability
Multiple large mismatched perfusion defects (wedge-shaped); normal ventilationHigh Probability
One or more mismatched defects, some uncertaintyIntermediate Probability
Matched defects (both V & Q abnormal); no mismatchesLow Probability
Normal perfusionNormal / PE excluded

PE Characteristics on Scan

  • Mismatched defect — normal ventilation, abnormal perfusion
  • Defects commonly wedge-shaped
  • Most common in lower lobes (clots travel from deep leg veins)
  • May occur anywhere; compare all views

COPD Characteristics on Scan

  • Matched defect — both V and Q abnormal
  • Defects are NOT wedge-shaped
  • ¹³³Xe washout: tracer trapping, delayed/absent clearance
  • Ventilation: patchy, non-uniform distribution
Whenever a lung scan is positive for any finding (regardless of diagnosis), the report typically reads "Low Probability of PE" — unless there are clear mismatched defects meeting high-probability criteria.

Lung Quantification

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16

Why Perform It?

  • Pre-surgical planning — predict post-operative lung function before lobectomy or pneumonectomy for lung cancer
  • Regional lung function assessment — how much does each lung contribute?
  • Asymmetry evaluation — detect under-performing lung/lobe due to obstruction, PE, or chronic disease

How It Is Done

  1. Complete a full V/Q scan (ventilation + perfusion)
  2. Draw a Region of Interest (ROI) around each lung
  3. Perform data analysis to calculate activity per lung/lobe

Normal Values

LungNormal %Lobar Breakdown (approx.)
Right lung~55%Upper: 20%, Middle: 12%, Lower: 23%
Left lung~45%Upper (incl. lingula): 20%, Lower: 25%
Right lung is larger (3 lobes) and contributes more. Left lung accommodates the heart (cardiac notch), making it smaller. This asymmetry is normal.

Five Lung Lobes (Know These)

  • Right Upper Lobe
  • Right Middle Lobe
  • Right Lower Lobe
  • Left Upper Lobe
  • Left Lower Lobe

Case Study Review

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16

How to Approach a Lung Scan Case

  1. Note patient history and clinical question (is this a PE evaluation?)
  2. Compare ventilation and perfusion — same view, side by side
  3. Identify any defects — are they on perfusion, ventilation, or both?
  4. Determine: mismatched (PE) or matched (COPD)?
  5. Note defect shape — wedge = PE indicator
  6. State probability (High / Intermediate / Low / Normal)

Case Summary Table

CasePatientKey FindingDiagnosis

222 YO male, post leg surgery, SOBMassive perfusion decrease in entire R lung — mismatchedMassive PE, R lung

427 YO female, sickle cell, tachypnea2 matched defects at L Post-BaseLow probability PE / COPD
545 YO male, SOB, only POST viewLarge matched defect, POST lobe L lungCOPD
680 YO male, chest painLarge round matched defect in R lung — unusual shapeLarge tumor
Remember: Round/perfectly circular matched defects are suspicious — ventilation defects are rarely perfectly round. Consider tumor or mass when shape is atypical for COPD.

Glossary

📘 NM&MI Tech · Ch. 16
V/Q Scan
Combined ventilation/perfusion lung scan; primary tool to evaluate for pulmonary embolism.
Mismatched Defect
Normal ventilation with abnormal perfusion — classic sign of PE. Ventilation = normal; Perfusion = defect.
Matched Defect
Both ventilation and perfusion show a defect in the same area — indicates COPD or obstructive airway disease.
Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
Blood clot in a pulmonary artery. Originates from DVT. Shows as mismatched, often wedge-shaped perfusion defect.
COPD
Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease. Matched defects on V/Q. Tracer trapping on ¹³³Xe washout. 80% from smoking.
¹³³Xe (Xenon-133)
Radioactive inert gas; most common ventilation agent. Three phases: wash-in, equilibrium, washout. POST view only.
⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA Aerosol
Radiolabeled aerosol ventilation agent. 30 mCi in nebulizer; 6 views; 2–10% reaches lungs.
⁹⁹ᵐTc-MAA
Macroaggregated Albumin; perfusion agent. 1–5 mCi IV. Particles (5–100 µm) lodge in pulmonary capillaries.
Capillary Blockade
Mechanism of MAA: particles (5–100 µm) block capillaries on first pass. 95% localizes in lungs.
Wash-in Phase
Phase 1 of ¹³³Xe: patient inhales Xe, holds 15 sec. Detects 66% of defects.
Equilibrium Phase
Phase 2 of ¹³³Xe: patient rebreathes Xe + O₂ for 2–5 min. Least sensitive; baseline for washout.
Washout Phase
Phase 3 of ¹³³Xe: patient breathes room air. Retention = COPD. Detects 90% of obstructive disease.
Lung Quantification
Study measuring % contribution of each lung/lobe. Normal: R ~55%, L ~45%. Used for pre-surgical planning.
Saddle PE
Massive PE where clot straddles bifurcation of main pulmonary artery; life-threatening.
Lobar PE
Clot in a lobar branch; blocks flow to one lung lobe.
Distal PE
Small peripheral clot; may cause pleuritic chest pain.
MAA Particle Count
Minimum 100,000 required; typically 200,000–700,000 injected. Size: 5–100 µm.
Nebulizer
Device that converts liquid ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA into a radiolabeled aerosol using oxygen at 10 L/min.
POST View
Most important view in lung scanning; least attenuation; only view taken with ¹³³Xe.
Pneumothorax
Air in pleural cavity; can cause atelectasis (lung collapse).
DVT
Deep Vein Thrombosis; most common source of pulmonary emboli.
Cardiac Notch
Concave area on the left lung where the heart lies; shows as attenuated area on lung scan — not a defect.
