Introduction to Generators Con't

Radionuclides have been used for many years to produce Radiopharmaceuticals

  • Short Lived Radionuclides have become common:
    • Does 99mTc sound Familiar?

Our previous lesson showed how Radionuclides are produced:

  • Obviously, hospitals are not able to have a Nuclear Reactor or a Particle Accelerator on hand
  • To bring short-lived Radionuclides to the hospitals, Nuclear Medicine Generators were introduced

This is a basic idea of how a generator works:

  • A parent Radionuclide decays
  • The decay results in a new radionuclide (daughter)

This is just a basic introduction; we will get more involved later

With Nuclear Medicine Generators:

  • No worries about short lived radionuclides
  • No worries about having to have reactors or accelerators

In general, a generator:

  • Weighs about 45 lbs
  • Is about 18 inches high and 6 inches wide
  • Is cylindrical shaped
  • Is housed inside lead
3

Generator Characteristics

Certain Characteristics are required for an Ideal Generator:

  1. Parent Radionuclide should be longer lived (longer T1/2) than daughter Radionuclide
    • Causes an equilibrium between Parent & Daughter
    • We'll talk more about equilibrium later
  2. Parent has long enough T1/2 for good generator life
    • If the parent has a short T1/2, the generator may only last a day or two
    • A week is a good length for generator life
  3. Allow separation of daughter and parent
    • When the daughter radionuclide is produced it has to be easily separated from the remaining parent radionuclide
    • Easy separation also means cheap
  4. Daughter must be a different element than the Parent
    • This makes the daughter Carrier Free
    • This also makes for separation from the parent easier
  5. The generator should be sterile and pyrogen free
    • A radionuclide that is not sterile or pyrogen free can cause adverse reactions
  6. Can elute the generator with a Physiologic pH solution
    • Physiologic pH is the pH of blood
    • If our solution is at physiologic pH it can mix with blood easily
  7. The generator should be easy to elute
    • Easy here also means quick
  8. Stable or very long T1/2 of granddaughter
    • When the Daughter decays, its product should be stable or very long-lived
    • This keeps patient exposure down since the patient is only receiving radiation from one radionuclide
  9. T1/2 of Parent permits good regrowth
    • The generator should decay to the daughter quick enough to allow you to elute your generator often
    • Being able to elute once per day means you have a good T1/2 for regrowth

Common Generators

These generators are listed as: Parent → Daughter → Granddaughter

  1. 99Mo → 99mTc → 99Tc
    • 99Mo has a half-life of 67 hours
    • 99mTc has a half-life of 6 hours
    • 99Tc has a half-life of 210,000 years
  2. 113Sn → 113mIn → 113In
    • 113Sn has a half-life of 115 days
    • 113mIn has a half-life of 1.7 hours
    • 113In is stable
  3. 81Rb → 81mKr → 81Kr
    • 81Rb has a half-life of 4.7 hours
    • 81mKr has a half-life of 13 seconds
    • 81Kr is stable

What do these generators have in common?

  • A Parent Radionuclide — What is loaded into the generator
  • A Daughter Radionuclide — What the parent decays to
    • This is the result of the generator that we take and use
  • A Granddaughter — What the daughter decays to — Is either stable or very long-lived

Types of Generators

There are 2 main types of Generators:

  • The type of generator depends on the physical state of the parent
    1. Liquid Column Generator
      • The parent is loaded into the generator in a liquid form
      • Advantages — Cheap
      • Disadvantages — Requires more extensive separation methods
        • More difficult to separate the daughter from the parent
      • Not used in Nuclear Medicine Anymore
    2. Solid Column Generator
      • The parent is loaded into the generator in a solid form
      • Advantages — Easy separation of the parent and daughter
      • Disadvantages — More expensive
      • Main type of generator used in Nuclear Medicine Today

From here on out we will focus on a Solid Column Generator

Generator Parts and Function

Parts of a Generator:

The whole generator system is in vacuum

Each individual part within this vacuum has its own function:

  1. Eluting Solvent
    • A physiologic pH solution
      • Normal saline, 0.9% NaCl, Isotonic Saline — 3 different names for the same solution
      • Contained in a large glass vial
      • A normal size generator uses 135 mL vials
      • The vial is fastened to the generator
      • Tubing is attached to the vial
      • The tubing takes the Eluting solvent to wash over the column
  2. Column
    • A glass cylinder positioned close to the center of the generator
  3. Absorbent Material
    • Stored inside the column
    • Alumina is the most common absorbent material used in a generator
    • The term Absorbent means to hold or take up
      • Absorbent material will hold or take up another material
    • Alumina will hold the parent radionuclide
  4. Filter
    • The filter is a Glass Fritted Disk
    • Filters the solution leaving the column
    • The alumina is primarily the only thing that needs to be filtered
  5. Lead
    • The entire column is shielded by lead
    • The generator itself is shielded by lead as well
  6. Elution
    • Your final product
    • Contains the daughter radionuclide + some of the eluting solvent
    • The elution is stored in a glass vial
7

Generator Function

In order to get a generator to work it must be filled:

  • The generator is filled with the parent radionuclide
    • We'll concentrate on the 99Mo/99mTc generator from here on out
  • 99Mo (the parent radionuclide) is loaded into the generator
    • Remember where the parent radionuclide goes in the generator
    • 99Mo gets adsorbed onto the alumina
      • Remember the alumina is stored inside the column
  • 99Mo decays to 99mTc
    • When Mo-99 decays, it is replaced by Tc-99m in a generator
      • Our goal is to extract the Tc-99m

Our first task is to get the generator started:

  • Our elution is stored in an evacuated vial
    • An evacuated vial means air has been sucked out of it
      • A 10cc(ml) evacuated vial has had 10cc of air sucked out of it
  • Remember that our generator system is in a closed system in vacuum
    • So, there is no air in our generator system

To start the generator:

  • Insert the elution vial (which is the evacuated vial) onto the needle of the generator system
  • The elution vial will immediately start to suck in
    • A 10cc evacuated vial will try to suck in 10cc of solution

What happens inside the column?

  • We have 99Mo
  • 99Mo decays to 99mTc
  • Our goal is to extract the 99mTc and leave the 99Mo inside the column

How do we extract the 99mTc and leave the 99Mo inside the column?

  • 99Mo is bound to the alumina by strong bonds
  • 99mTc on the other hand is bound to the alumina by a weak bond

This makes it easy to extract the 99mTc and preserve the 99Mo

Once the saline reaches the column it spreads out and travels throughout the column:

  • Saline is made up of Sodium (Na) and Chloride (Cl)
  • The sodium will grab hold of the weakly bound 99mTc
  • The Cl will replace the spot where the 99mTc was
  • Throughout this process the 99Mo should remain bound to the alumina

Our final product after the solution leaves the column is:

  • Sodium Pertechnetate
  • Na99mTcO4 travels through the filter (filters out the alumina)
  • Na99mTcO4 travels through the tubing
  • Na99mTcO4 then fills up the elution vial

The generator functions by:

  1. Insert the elution vial onto the generator
  2. NaCl is the eluting solvent
  3. NaCl washes over the column
  4. Inside the column Moly decays to Tech
  5. Na grabs the 99mTc
  6. Cl replaces where the 99mTc ion was
  7. 99Mo remains bound to the alumina
  8. Na99mTcO4 flows through the tubing and fills up the elution vial

Generator Elution

When should you elute the Moly/Tech generator?

  • 99Mo constantly decays to 99mTc
  • 99mTc also constantly decays to 99Tc
  • When you elute a generator you get both 99mTc and 99Tc
    • That's why it's important to elute your generator at certain intervals
  • After you elute the generator:
    • All of the 99mTc is washed out of the generator
    • 99Mo begins to decay to 99mTc again
    • It takes 23 hours for the generator to build up the maximum amount of 99mTc
  • At 23 hours you have the maximum amount of 99mTc
  • After the 23 hours you don't accumulate more 99mTc:
    • 99Mo is still decaying to 99mTc
    • However, 99mTc is also still decaying to 99Tc
  • That's why it's important to elute your generator every 23 hours
    • Most hospitals elute their generator once a day
      • Usually ends up being every 24 hours to keep it consistent
  • Another important factor to remember is that Eluted Na99mTcO4 expires in 12 hours after elution

Generator Equilibrium

Generator Equilibrium occurs when the Parent T1/2 is greater than Daughter T1/2

  • After a certain amount of time
  • The activity of the parent and daughter will be equal or close to being equal
  • The ratio of the parent to daughter becomes equal
  • The daughter's activity appears to be following the T1/2 of the parent
    • This is due to the rate that the daughter is being produced

There are 2 types of Generator Equilibrium:

  • The type depends on the T1/2 difference between the parent and daughter
  1. Secular Equilibrium — Parent's T1/2 is much larger than the daughter
    • Parent's T1/2 is usually 100 times larger or more than the daughters
    • 81Rb/81mKr and 113Sn/113mIn are examples of this generator
  2. Transient Equilibrium — Parent's T1/2 is not much larger than the daughter
    • Parent's T1/2 is usually 10–50 times larger than the daughters
    • 99Mo/99mTc is an example of this generator
      • It takes approximately 24 hours for a 99Mo/99mTc generator to reach equilibrium

Equilibrium Time Activity Curve

  • At initial activity and after each elution, the 99mTc activity is approximately 0.
  • From the time of elution, it takes 1 day (24 hours) to reach equilibrium
  • Equilibrium can be identified on the curve as the area where 99Mo and 99mTc appear to be decaying at the same rate

Concerning 99Mo/99mTc equilibrium:

  • At initial activity and after every elution, the 99mTc activity is approximately 0 mCi
  • It took about 24 hours to reach equilibrium
  • At Equilibrium:
    • The activity of the 99Mo & 99mTc are almost equal
    • 99Mo & 99mTc appear to be decaying at the same rate

With all this, we also say that when a generator reaches equilibrium it has reached maximum activity!

Moly Efficiency & Generator Size

Moly Efficiency

99Mo has an efficiency in a 99Mo/99mTc generator:

  • The 99Mo efficiency formula:
  • 99Mo efficiency is 88%
    • 88% of 99Mo decays to 99mTc
    • 12% of 99Mo decays to 99Tc
  • Both 99mTc & 99Tc are eluted in a generator
    • Remember the longer you wait to elute your generator the more 99mTc has decayed to 99Tc

Generator Efficiency

The 99Mo/99mTc generator has an efficiency as well:

  • It's also referred to as the yield of the generator
  • The 99Mo/99mTc generator efficiency formula:
  • 99Mo/99mTc generator efficiency is 85–95%
    • 85–95% of 99mTc in the column is eluted
    • 5–15% of the 99mTc remains on the column

Now that you know all about a generator, who uses them?

  • Any hospital can use a 99Mo/99mTc generator:
    • Today they are usually only seen in the larger hospitals
    • Smaller hospitals tend to use "Unit Doses" supplied by a Nuclear Pharmacy
  • A Nuclear Pharmacy can use a 99Mo/99mTc generator:
    • A Nuclear Pharmacy is a centralized pharmacy that can supply 99mTc and other radiopharmaceuticals to hospitals
    • The Nuclear Pharmacy supplies the "Unit Doses"

Generator Size

When we refer to the size of a 99Mo/99mTc generator:

  • The amount of 99Mo loaded into the column of a generator
    • Hospitals use generator sizes of 0.22–3 Ci of 99Mo
    • Pharmacies use generator sizes of 8.3–16.6 Ci of 99Mo
  • The amount of 99Mo in a generator is recorded at calibration
    • Calibration is the day of shipping of the generator from the manufacturer

Generator Information

Certain information is required to be listed on a 99Mo/99mTc generator:

  • Amount of 99Mo in the generator at calibration
  • Date and time of calibration
  • When the generator expires

  2. No particle emission from Tc99m

Technetium-99m

Now that we have our elution from a 99Mo/99mTc generator we can talk about:

  • Since 99mTc is used in 80% of nuclear medicine studies, we need to know all about it
  • There are 3 main contents in a 99Mo/99mTc generator elution

What do we need to talk about with Technetium-99m?

  • General Properties and characteristics
  • Oxidation states of Technetium-99m
  • Reduction of Technetium-99m
  • Free Pertechnetium in a Radiopharmaceutical kit
  • Hydrolysis of Reduced Tc-99m and Tin
  • Bound Tc-99m

What we know about Technetium

  • Atomic Number — 43
  • There are 22 isotopes of Tc with mass #'s ranging from 90–111
  • All Isotopes of Tc are Radioactive — No stable isotopes
  • Comes from the Greek word technetos meaning artificial

What we know about Technetium-99m

  • Atomic Number — 43
  • Has a 6 hour Half-Life
  • Has an abundant 140 keV gamma ray
    • 90% of 99mTc decay by 140 keV gamma ray
  • Easily available in the Moly/Tech generator
  • Eluted as sterile, pyrogen and carrier free

Oxidation States of Tc99m

Before we get into the oxidation states of 99mTc, let's figure out what oxidation is:

  • Oxidation of an element is any reaction in which electrons are transferred
    • Electrons are transferred between 2 elements
  • Oxidation # is the # of protons compared to electrons
    • An element with a 7+ oxidation # means it has 7 more protons than electrons
    • Oxidation # is also referred to as the oxidation state

99mTc can exist in 8 oxidation states (1- to 7+):

  • 99mTc at 7+ means it has 7 more protons than electrons — needs 7 electrons to bond with another element
  • 99mTc at 1- means it has 1 more electron than protons — needs to donate 1 electron to bond

Reduction of 99mTc

When 99mTc is eluted from a generator it is at a 7+ oxidation state:

  • 99mTc7+ can be reduced to one of its other 7 oxidation states
  • Reducing 99mTc to a lower oxidation state will add e- to 99mTc:
    • 99mTc7+ + 3e- → 99mTc4+
    • 99mTc7+ + 4e- → 99mTc3+

99mTc7+ is not good for labeling with other compounds — must be reduced first.

Reducing agents that can reduce 99mTc7+:

  • Stannous Chloride, Stannous Tartrate, HCl, Sodium Borohydride & Ferrous Sulfate
  • Stannous Chloride (SnCl) is the most common reducing agent

The ratio of SnCl to 99mTc7+ is 106 : 1 — ensuring complete reduction.

Common oxidation states to reduce 99mTc7+ to:

  • 99mTc5+, 99mTc3+, 99mTc1+
  • Each oxidation state labels to different compounds to form different radiopharmaceuticals

Free 99mTc in a Radiopharmaceutical Kit

Free 99mTc = 99mTc that is NOT bound to a pharmaceutical. It won't go to the target organ.

Causes of Free 99mTc:

  1. 99mTc7+ that did not get reduced (most common reason)
    • Oxygen in the vial will oxidize the SnCl and render it useless
    • Not enough SnCl in the vial to reduce the 99mTc
  2. Oxygen in the vial causing the 99mTc-pharmaceutical bond to break

Prevention: keep enough SnCl in the kit and keep oxygen and air out of the kit.

Hydrolysis of Reduced 99mTc

  • Reduced 99mTc can react with water instead of the pharmaceutical — rendering the agent ineffective
  • Tin (Sn) can also undergo hydrolysis — losing its ability to reduce 99mTc

Bound 99mTc in a Radiopharmaceutical Kit

  • Bound 99mTc is good — it is 99mTc bound to a radiopharmaceutical
  • Examples: 99mTc-HDP, 99mTc-Sestamibi, 99mTc-Mebrofenin

Decreased Yield

What would cause you to have less 99mTc Elution than you expected?

  1. Channeling (most common reason)
    • When part of the column is not accessible to the eluting solution
    • Usually caused by an air pocket on the column
      • Prevents the NaCl from washing over that section of the column
      • If NaCl doesn't wash over a section, the 99mTc will not be eluted from that section
  2. Less 99Mo loaded onto the column
    • Less 99Mo creates less 99mTc to be eluted
  3. Clogged tubing
    • Prevents some or all of the 99mTc from traveling through the tubing & into the elution vial
  4. Loss of Vacuum
    • Usually caused by holes or cracks in the tubing or glass column
    • Air enters → vacuum is gone → Na99mTcO4 is not pulled into the elution vial
  5. Cracks in the Elution vial
    • A crack allows air to be sucked in → evacuation is lost
    • Na99mTcO4 will not be sucked into the elution vial

Glossary

Generator
A device containing a parent radionuclide that decays to produce a useful daughter radionuclide. Typically ~45 lbs, 18 inches tall, cylindrical, housed in lead.
Parent Radionuclide
The longer-lived radionuclide loaded into a generator that decays to produce the daughter. In the Moly/Tech generator, 99Mo is the parent.
Daughter Radionuclide
The product of parent decay extracted for clinical use. In the Moly/Tech generator, 99mTc is the daughter.
Granddaughter
The stable or very long-lived product of daughter decay. In the Moly/Tech generator, 99Tc (T½ = 210,000 years) is the granddaughter.
Elution
The process of washing the generator column with saline to extract the daughter radionuclide. Also refers to the final product (Na99mTcO4) collected in the evacuated vial.
Eluting Solvent
Normal saline (0.9% NaCl, isotonic saline) — the physiologic pH solution used to wash over the column and extract 99mTc.
Alumina
The absorbent material inside the column that holds the parent radionuclide (99Mo) via strong bonds while allowing 99mTc to be eluted via weak bonds.
Carrier Free
A daughter radionuclide that is a different element than its parent, making separation easy and eliminating contamination with the parent.
Physiologic pH
The pH of blood. The eluting solvent must be at physiologic pH so the final product can safely mix with blood when administered to patients.
Evacuated Vial
A glass vial from which air has been removed. When inserted onto the generator needle, the vacuum pulls the elution solution through the column and into the vial.
Sodium Pertechnetate
Na99mTcO4 — the final product of generator elution. Expires 12 hours after elution.
Generator Equilibrium
The state reached when the ratio of parent to daughter activity becomes constant. Occurs when parent T½ is greater than daughter T½.
Secular Equilibrium
Generator equilibrium where the parent T½ is ≥100× larger than the daughter. Examples: 81Rb/81mKr, 113Sn/113mIn.
Transient Equilibrium
Generator equilibrium where the parent T½ is 10–50× larger than the daughter. Example: 99Mo/99mTc (takes ~24 hours to reach).
Moly Efficiency
The fraction of 99Mo that decays to 99mTc — 88%. The remaining 12% decays directly to 99Tc.
Generator Efficiency (Yield)
The fraction of 99mTc in the column that is actually eluted — 85–95%. Also called generator yield.
Calibration
The day the generator is shipped from the manufacturer. The amount of 99Mo is recorded at this time.
Unit Dose
A pre-measured dose of radiopharmaceutical supplied by a Nuclear Pharmacy to smaller hospitals that do not have their own generators.
Oxidation State
The number of protons compared to electrons in an element. 99mTc can exist in 8 oxidation states from 1- to 7+. Eluted as 7+.
Reduction (of 99mTc)
Adding electrons to 99mTc7+ to lower its oxidation state (e.g., to 4+, 3+, or 1+), allowing it to bond with a pharmaceutical.
Stannous Chloride (SnCl)
The most common reducing agent used to reduce 99mTc7+ in a radiopharmaceutical kit. Added at a ratio of 106 : 1.
Free 99mTc
99mTc not bound to a pharmaceutical in a kit. Most commonly caused by 99mTc7+ that was not reduced. Will not localize to the target organ.
Hydrolysis
A reaction in which reduced 99mTc or Sn reacts with water rather than the pharmaceutical, rendering the agent ineffective.
Channeling
The most common cause of decreased generator yield. An air pocket prevents saline from washing part of the column, leaving 99mTc trapped.
Loss of Vacuum
A cause of decreased yield where cracks or holes in tubing or the glass column allow air to enter, eliminating the vacuum needed to collect the elution.
