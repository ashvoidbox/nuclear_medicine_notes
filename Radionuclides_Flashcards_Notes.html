How to Use These Flashcards

Each card shows the radionuclide name on the front. Tap or click the card to flip it and reveal the half-life, photon energy, and decay mode on the back.

Use the Flip All button at the top of each group to reveal or hide all cards at once. Use the Quiz button in the sidebar to test yourself with 10 random multiple-choice questions.

Cards are grouped by clinical use: SPECT agents, PET agents, Therapy agents, and Calibration sources.

Glossary

Half-Life (T½)
The time required for half of the radioactive atoms in a sample to decay. Shorter T½ = faster decay and lower patient dose retention.
Isomeric Transition (IT)
Decay from a metastable nuclear state (e.g., 99mTc) to a lower energy state by emitting a gamma photon. No change in atomic or mass number.
Beta− Decay
A neutron converts to a proton with emission of a beta particle (electron) and antineutrino. Increases atomic number by 1.
Electron Capture (EC)
An orbital electron is captured by the nucleus; a proton converts to a neutron. Characteristic X-rays are emitted. Decreases atomic number by 1.
Positron Decay (β+)
A proton converts to a neutron with emission of a positron. The positron annihilates with an electron to produce two 511 keV photons traveling 180° apart — the basis of PET imaging.
Alpha Decay
Emission of an alpha particle (2 protons + 2 neutrons). High LET, short range — useful for targeted therapy (e.g., Radium-223). Decreases atomic number by 2 and mass by 4.
Annihilation Radiation
The two 511 keV photons produced when a positron meets an electron. All PET agents produce this energy regardless of which positron emitter is used.
SPECT
Single-Photon Emission Computed Tomography. Uses a gamma camera to detect single gamma photons from radionuclides like 99mTc, 201Tl, and 67Ga.
PET
Positron Emission Tomography. Detects the two 511 keV coincidence photons from positron annihilation. Requires positron-emitting radionuclides.
Calibration Source
A long-lived radionuclide (e.g., 57Co, 137Cs) used to verify the accuracy and linearity of gamma cameras and dose calibrators.
Metastable State
A nuclear isomer with excess energy that decays by isomeric transition. Denoted by a lowercase "m" after the mass number (e.g., 99mTc).
keV (kiloelectron volt)
Unit of photon energy. Optimal gamma camera imaging range is ~80–364 keV. Energies too low are attenuated; too high are difficult to collimate.
